The Technology-Playful Probe

U&S C&A T&R M,D&C

Advanced technologies are reshaping the way we work, learn, and live, including our research practices. Beyond acting as research tools, technologies have the potential to function as research collaborators and even "synthetic users" for early testing.

While current literature covers the use of IoT in research and starts to show results for the use of AI, there is a significant gap regarding the possible effect of Human Behavioural Digital Twins (HBDTs) as a research collaborator, and how our methods might integrate such a technology.

This two-study project (M1.2 & M2.1) explores and validates the Technology-Playful Probe (TPP), a method designed for HBDTs that combines Technology Probes, Playful Probes, and Experience Sampling to collect richer, real-time user data and contextual insights.

Project Information:

  • Institution: Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e)
  • Course: M1.2 Research Project / M2.1 FMP Preparation
  • Year: 2025

What did I learn...

Across M1.2 and M2.1, I worked on the Technology Playful Probe (TPP) project while developing my design research practice and exploring my future direction in R&D and HCI.

In M1.2, I focused on building the TPP and its dashboard, developing a foundational understanding of data analysis methods such as descriptive statistics and inductive thematic analysis. The process pushed me to switch between Design, System, and Algorithmic Thinking, translating concepts into working system logic and implementation. When technical limitations arose, I adapted by learning new tools such as MongoDB, React, and Render, which strengthened my ability to iterate, integrate systems, and work more resiliently.

In M2.1, I further refined the method by critically integrating earlier insights and expanding my knowledge of evaluation and validation techniques. I worked with real-time data, improved the TPP system to better capture user input, and involved experts to validate the approach. This phase also gave me exposure to academic research through close collaboration with a PhD researcher and participation in a conference, helping me better understand research practice in context.

Overall, this two-study project significantly strengthened my design research, programming, analytical, and methodological skills, and clarified my career direction. While I initially considered pursuing a PhD, this experience helped me conclude that it is not the path I want to take for now and instead confirmed my intention to pursue industry-focused R&D as a UX/UI designer grounded in research and hands-on exploration.
