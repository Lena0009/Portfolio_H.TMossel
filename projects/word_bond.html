Word Bond

Word Bond is an AI-driven assistive technology designed to help visually impaired parents read aloud to their sighted children, preserving the intimacy of shared storytelling. It addresses accessibility barriers in children’s books by combining OCR, LLM-based text correction, and text-to-speech to support natural, voice-led reading.

Parents scan a book in advance, after which the system converts and corrects the text into audio prompts that guide them during reading. In interactive mode, hand tracking allows children to point at words, triggering word recognition and optional audio feedback for the parent. Designed with simple controls and audio cues, Word Bond aims to make reading together more accessible while maintaining a personal parent–child connection.

Project Information:

  • Institution: Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e)
  • Course: Designing with Advanced Artificial Intelligence (DBM180)
  • Year: 2024
  • Team: Jesse Strijker, Zhaopeng Zhu, Helena Mossel

What did I learn...

I took this course to explore machine learning and its use in interactive projects, and to understand whether it is a direction I want to pursue.

Through lectures, self-study, and the experimentation stage of the group project, I built a basic understanding of ML concepts and gained hands-on experience with OCR, text-to-speech, and model integration. I also implemented a finger-tracking model, which improved my Python skills and showed me how accessible these tools can be with proper documentation and peer support. A key insight was learning to critically evaluate when and why to use AI in user-centered design, rather than applying it by default.

My role focused mainly on concept development, evaluation, and user testing analysis, which strengthened my ability to reflect on the meaningful use of ML and structure evaluations. However, I also see it as a missed opportunity to be more involved in building the final prototype and further develop my technical implementation skills.
