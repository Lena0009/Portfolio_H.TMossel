The Interactive Book

C&A T&R M,D&C

The Interactive Book explores a screenless tangible user interface (STUI) for children under four, responding to concerns about early screen exposure and its impact on development. Using computer vision and ArUco markers, it transforms a physical book into an interactive system where children engage through touch, movement, and physical objects instead of screens.

The prototype includes two pages: music-making and storytelling. In the music activity, children use tangible blocks to explore and modify sound through simple gestures, supporting sensory exploration and collaboration. In storytelling, a loop-based object is used to “scan” the page and trigger narrative sounds, encouraging independent discovery or shared interaction with parents.

Project Information:

  • Institution: Eindhoven University of Technology (TU/e)
  • Course: Designing User Interfaces with Emerging Technologies (DBM170)
  • Year: 2024

What did I learn...

During this course’s project I learned that UI design is deeply rooted in Human-Computer Interaction, from traditional WIMP systems to post-WIMP interfaces, and explored technologies such as VR and computer vision markers as accessible tools for future projects.

This project helped me improve my technical skills by shifting from a “build everything at once” approach to working iteratively in sprints, which strengthened both my prototype and my design process. I also gained practical experience in programming, Blender, and 3D printing.

While I met my learning goals, seeing my peers’ work made me realise I could have experimented more freely with CV markers, which motivates a more exploratory mindset in future projects. Overall, the course reinforced my interest in the relationship between humans and technology and my intention to further specialise in this area.
